# Explore route — create the first character identity

Use this route only when no character image has human approval. Find a simple,
lovable identity; do not finish a logo system.

## Propose directions

When the user has not fixed a subject, derive three genuinely different
character subjects or metaphors from distinct product behaviors or emotional
promises. When the user fixed a subject, keep it and propose three materially
different treatments through silhouette, defining feature, broad secondary
region, or personality emphasis.

Describe each direction in one line:

```text
<character subject> — <product connection> — <defining silhouette> — <main false-friend risk>
```

The proven default planning shape is six independent candidates: two per
direction. Assign composition from each direction's subject, silhouette, and
target job so the pair explores materially different spatial relationships.
Do not make mirrored lower corners, centered portraits, or any other single
layout the default. Treat six calls as a proposal, not automatic spend. Follow
an explicit user quantity or distribution and record the override.

Wait for the human to approve the directions and exact call count unless the
current request already authorizes them.

## Apply the character budget

- Build one dominant continuous outer silhouette from roughly `4–7` large soft
  shapes.
- Keep at most one defining paired feature, such as two rounded ears or two broad
  leaves, and preserve both members visibly.
- Use at most two broad internal character-color regions by default plus one
  solid background. Follow an explicit palette or color-count request.
- Keep the face to two simple eyes and one tiny mouth only when useful.
- Remove highlights, texture, outlines, anatomy, accessories, repeated detail,
  thin gaps, sharp tips, and decoration that does not carry recognition.
- Require a bold silhouette and readable expression at `32 × 32`.

Use thick rounded contours, compact proportions, soft cheeks, widely spaced eyes,
and a calm friendly posture. Ask only for extremely subtle depth. Do not default
to clay, plush, inflatable, glossy plastic, photorealistic material, or dramatic
three-dimensional rendering.

## Choose composition for the direction

Give every candidate one explicit placement and negative-space job. Centered,
side-cropped, high/low, corner-emergent, symmetrical, and strongly asymmetric
arrangements are all available. Choose the arrangement that strengthens the
subject and product connection rather than filling a preselected grid pattern.

Use intentional cropping when it strengthens the silhouette, but keep the face
and defining paired feature visible. If a later surface uses a rounded mask,
keep load-bearing identity away from unsafe edges. Across a batch, vary spatial
relationships deliberately; do not produce six versions of the same portrait
or force every direction into opposite corners.

## Prompt skeleton

Adapt this skeleton to each approved direction. Describe an image, not a logo or
app icon.

```text
Create one complete full-bleed 1:1 square image.
Background: fill the square with solid <background>. Keep it visible in every open area not occupied by the character and preserve the assigned negative-space relationship.
Subject: place one extremely simplified, cute, endearing <subject> character on the background, reduced to one soft rounded continuous silhouette and one defining feature tied to <product connection>.
Complexity: use only 4–7 large basic shapes and at most two broad internal character-color regions. Use two simple eyes and one tiny mouth only when it helps. Remove nonessential lines, anatomical detail, texture, and decoration. Keep the character readable at 32 × 32.
Color behavior: use <two character color families> plus the solid background as broad purposeful semantic masses. Reuse character colors for facial marks. Keep the silhouette, face, defining feature, and background clearly separated.
Composition: use this exact direction-owned arrangement: <assigned placement, scale, crop, and negative-space relationship>. Preserve paired identifying features and the complete face. Do not substitute a generic centered portrait or force a corner treatment unless that is the assigned composition.
Style: make simplification, cuteness, and lovable baby-like appeal decisive. Use large soft forms, compact proportions, thick rounded contours, and an ultra-clean graphic treatment. Add an extremely, extremely subtle, almost imperceptible sense of depth through a barely-there neo-skeuomorphic treatment.
Finish: show only the character on the full-canvas background with normal square outer corners.
Constraints: no text, watermark, border, frame, card, mockup, extra subject, scenery, fragile line, sharp tip, tiny decoration, glossy hotspot, deep occlusion, extrusion, strong three-dimensional rendering, or external cast shadow. Keep the background solid and uniform.
```

## Present and lock

Generate each candidate once with no image reference. Preserve every return and
show one equal-size board before agent ranking. Ask the human to select one or
more labels and state what worked. Do not start Adapt until one exact raw image
is selected as the identity authority.
